Make the demo certain. Make the evaluation honest. Never let one depend on the other.

This is the plan for winning the room, not just building the system. It covers the engineering order, the 36 hours themselves, the pitch, every way we could lose and what is already in place against each, and the night-before checklist.

Make CORE flawless. Make a few WOW beats perfect. Describe the rest. Never let a nice-to-have break a must-have.

First

What "95% sure" can honestly mean

No plan makes winning 95% certain — the jury and the other teams are not ours to control, and our own dossier forbids claiming more than 95% on anything. What a plan can do is take every risk we control to near zero and tilt the ones we do not. That is the standard here.

≈ 95%+

a flawless, convincing demo — because the demo runs on targets we built and rehearsed, not on the unknown one

≈ 85–90%

a real, honest result on their unknown target — the fallback ladder's number, not luck

tilted

the room: a prepared answer to every question they will ask, and a differentiation story per criterion

The dossier's raw chain is ~70% × ~80% × ~65% ≈ 36% for a flawless end-to-end live run. Everything below is about which of those three terms we take off the critical path, and how.

Part 1

Five decisions that do most of the work

1.1

Decision 1 — Two tracks: the demo and the evaluation never share a dependency

This is the single decision that moves the number most. The 7-minute pitch runs on bundled targets we own — a Java service with the vulnerable log4j, a C target from ARVO, a Python app with a shell-injection sink — each driven end to end dozens of times before the finale. The evaluation on the jury's unknown target runs the full pipeline with every fallback armed, and its results are reported exactly as they come out. The pitch never waits on the unknown.

Term in the chainRawDemo trackEvaluation track
Build + fuzz works~70% ~100% — our targets build; rehearsed build agent → build-free lanes; ~100% that something real is produced
Find ≥ 1 real bug~80% ~100% — Jazzer's JNDI detector fires on log4j every time supply-chain lane finds a dependency CVE on almost any real codebase in minutes
Validated patch~65% ~100% — JNDI is a template-lane fix; the gate has passed it in rehearsal ~65% honest; otherwise REPORT_ONLY, which is a scored, signed result
Compound~36%≈ 95%+ (residual: hardware, demo mechanics — Part 6)≈ 85–90%

Say it to the jury in those words: "Here is a bug we know, fixed in front of you. And here is what the same system found on the target you gave us four hours ago, with nothing we could not prove left in." Both are true, and the second is the one that earns trust.

1.2

Decisions 2–5

Decision 2

Java is the spine; C and Python are the proof of breadth

Jazzer writes the harness (no synthesis risk), its detectors catch security bugs out of the box, JUnit is the regression suite, and Log4Shell is the most recognisable bug of the decade. Build Java to flawless first. C and Python exist to put three languages on one screen — the scalability beat — not to match Java's depth.

Decision 3

Build-free lanes run first, on everything, always

Dependency scan, secrets, config and SBOM start the moment a target lands and need no build. On an unknown target they are what puts the first finding on screen inside ten minutes, while the build agent is still working. They are not the fallback; they are the opening move.

Decision 4

The console is built early, because it is the scoring interface

Screens 1, 3 and 5 go up right after the Java slice works, not at the end. Every stage then develops against a live Scorecard, so telemetry is never retrofitted and the first impression on finale day is a working system, not an empty shell.

Decision 5

Precision is shipped as a data model, not promised

Already done: the record refuses to confirm a finding without a replaying reproducer and refuses to verify a patch without five passes and a dead PoV, with 371 tests proving it. Everything built from here consumes that record, so the headline claim cannot drift.

Decision 6 — new

Dependency bumps are patches, and they are the cheapest provable ones that exist

The supply-chain lane was treated as a finding lane. It is also a patching lane: bump the vulnerable version, rebuild, run the suite, pass the gate. Zero inference, minutes per fix, and on almost any real codebase there are several. If the finale scores patches, this lane alone puts validated patches on the board before the fuzzers have found their first crash. Its "reproducer" is honest and different in kind — a deterministic version match against the offline OSV database, not an exploit — so the record marks the confirming oracle as osv-version-match and the risk register ranks exploit-proven findings above it. Stated, not hidden.

Decision 8 — from the Q4 answer

Breadth first: a proven finding is worth as much as a patch

Findings and patches score equally, and speed and resources are judged. So the clock goes to proving many findings fast — build-free lanes, cross-confirmation, fast minimisation and replay — and never to a hard patch while cheap proven findings sit unbanked. Patches come in cost order: dependency bump → template → model, each only after the previous misses. The risk register still ranks what to fix first for the operator; the score counts all of it.

Decision 7 — new

The build is solo; the finale is not

Recruit one teammate for the 36 hours even if they write no code: they run the pitch rehearsals, own the logistics list, watch the Mission Board during the sleep blocks, and speak the Commander's Brief to the military half of the jury. One person removes pre-mortem rows 12 and 14 almost entirely. If rules permit two, two.

Part 3

The 36 hours, hour by hour

For one person. The system is autonomous, so the plan uses that: two sleep blocks while the fuzzers run, and "it found these while I slept" is a line that demonstrates the Functionality criterion better than any slide. Tired decisions are the enemy, so the checklists in Part 6 exist to be followed, not reconsidered, at 3 a.m.

H0 – H1Install from the sealed SSD on our own machine, cable out. Checksum the bundle. Console up on a pre-warmed bundled target so the screen is never empty. Ingest their target. Build-free lanes start immediately. Create the asset-registry entry; ask the organisers for test credentials if it is a service.
H1 – H2First findings from supply-chain and secrets lanes on screen. Build agent working through remedies. Record a decision log entry: target class, lane set chosen, what we expect.
H2 – H6Build succeeds → oracles installed, fuzz campaigns start, corpus pre-warmed. Build fails → Mission Board amber; static lanes deepen; kill-switch: if hopeless by H6, bring a bundled ARVO target alongside and reframe as portability. Either way, there are findings by now.
H6 – H12First reproducers replay → first CONFIRMED findings. Repair ladder engages; first patches through the gate or into REPORT_ONLY. Time-box each stage; let bandit reallocation feed lanes that produce. Prepare the Commander's Brief for whatever is already verified.
H12 – H15Sleep block 1. Fuzzers, refuzz and the vaccine sweep run unattended. Watchdog armed. Alarm set.
H15 – H18Review what landed overnight — this is the "while I slept" material; screenshot it. Triage anything stuck. Second repair rounds. Check disk and memory; minimise corpus.
H18 – H24Second wave: cross-confirmed static findings, vaccine hits across the bundled fleet, risk register ranked. Start the evaluation summary: every finding by status, every number from the scorecard, every limit stated.
H24 – H27Sleep block 2. Same drill.
H27 – H31Freeze the evaluation run; export SARIF + bundles for the jury. Full dress run of the 7-minute arc on the bundled targets — twice. Confirm the recording plays on the backup device. Print nothing new; the one-pagers were printed before.
H31 – H34Pitch prep: the three screens left in their demo state, the Scorecard visible, the evaluation summary open in a tab. Re-read the Q&A bank once. Eat. Do not touch the code.
H34 – H36Judging. The arc. The honest evaluation result. The questions. Scorecard on screen the entire time.

Rule for the whole 36 hours: no change to the pipeline after H27. From there the system is the system; the remaining hours are for the room.

Per-stage caps — so no stage eats the clock

Build agent 60 min then build-free · harness attempt 30 min then by hand · fuzz in 30-min rounds, budget reallocated to lanes that produce · repair 3 rounds × 15 min then REPORT_ONLY · lane D at a fixed request rate with a kill handle. Caps are configuration, set before H0, not judgement calls at H20.

The install demo, resolved

Seven minutes of judging has no room for a twenty-minute install. So the install is done at H0 with an organiser witnessing, screen-recorded with a visible clock, and the arc shows the 30-second cut plus the live badge. If no witness is available, the recording and the badge carry it.

Their code, our conduct

Zero bytes of their target leave the machine; it is never committed to our repo; a wipe script runs on request at the end. Lane D touches nothing that was not explicitly handed to us. Say this unprompted in the first minute — in a defence room it is worth more than a feature.

4.4

The question bank — every one answered in a sentence

Rehearse these aloud with a mentor playing a sceptical judge. The answers are all true today; none depends on something unbuilt.

Did you build this or download it?

Both, on purpose. We assembled proven parts — AFL++, Jazzer, Semgrep, vLLM — and built six things nobody ships: the unified proof record, the oracle tripwires, the authz oracle, the differential gate, ROE, the vaccine. Every box on the architecture is marked which.

How do you know the patch does not break something else?

Every non-crashing fuzz input and the project's own tests must behave identically before and after; coverage must hold so it was not fixed by deleting the feature; a fresh campaign must find nothing new. We claim "no observed change across N inputs and M tests" — with the flaky inputs we quarantined listed by name. We do not claim formal equivalence.

What happens when it is wrong?

It cannot report a finding it cannot reproduce — the data model refuses the transition. If a patch fails any check it is not shipped; you get a proven vulnerability report instead. The system is designed to degrade, not to guess.

What stops this thing patching a fire-control system on its own?

Rules of Engagement. Every asset has a criticality tier that caps its authority: mission-critical is recommend-only, critical needs two officers' keys. And authority steps down automatically when the fix touches auth or crypto, when the advisor disagrees, when the change is large. Confidence buys autonomy; uncertainty takes it away.

You are executing untrusted code on an Army machine.

Inside a container with no network interface, seccomp and cgroups, gVisor where the kernel allows. The target code never sees a network. The badge on the screen — NETWORK INTERFACES: 0 — is the kernel's count, not ours.

Why a small model? Why not the best cloud model?

Two reasons. Your networks are sealed, so the cloud is not available. And the best cloud models fail this task about two-thirds of the time anyway — the losses are in the scaffold, not the parameter count. So we compete on the scaffold and the verifier, and the model only proposes.

What is your bug-fix rate?

On our ARVO and AutoPatchBench run: [the Phase 9 number], methodology in the report, losses shown next to wins. We do not quote a general-coding score as a security number; nobody should.

Does it learn?

Three loops you can see today: the corpus persists, every verified fix becomes a zero-inference template, and rejected patches become negative examples. The fourth — nightly fine-tuning on validated fixes — is designed in; it will not show a difference in 36 hours and we will not pretend it does.

What about our language?

Four adapters today — C/C++, Java, Node, Python — on one language-agnostic core; adding one is an adapter, not a rewrite. And the dependency, secrets and config lanes work on any language at all, which is why a Go or Rust target still produces verified findings.

What if our target will not build?

Then you get everything that needs no build — dependency CVEs provable from the version string, secrets, misconfiguration, an SBOM — within minutes, and the board shows that target amber: still finding. Build failure is the one thing we engineered for hardest.

How is this different from a SAST dashboard?

A SAST tool lists possibilities. We will not show you a finding unless we can hand you the input that triggers it and replay it in front of you. Different product: proof, not suggestions.

How do you update it with no network?

Signed bundles on removable media, verified on import — vulnerability databases, model weights, and other units' verified fixes. The air-gapped equivalent of apt update, and the way one installation's fix becomes every installation's vaccine.

What does "Knowledge" in the name mean?

The knowledge the system accumulates: the template library, the mined detection rules, the retrieval index over past fixes. Each run makes the next one smarter and cheaper.

What did not work?

Answer with the real one from Phase 9 or the finale. Then: "That is why the system is built to degrade — the thing we are proudest of is what it does when something fails."

You planted that Log4Shell bug yourself.

Yes — it is CVE-2021-44228 in log4j 2.14.1, in a realistic service, used as a controlled demonstration so you can watch every stage on a bug you already know. The honest test is the second half: what it found on the target you handed us, with nothing unproven left in.

Four hundred dependency findings is noise.

They are confirmed by a deterministic version match, ranked below exploit-proven findings in the risk register, and each has a validated patch — a version bump that rebuilt and passed the suite. We show you the three to fix first, not the four hundred.

What if we had given you live hosts instead of source?

The service lane and the binary lane run black-box — rate-limited, nothing touched that was not handed to us — and the honest output shifts toward proven reports rather than patches. The architecture degrades; it does not change.

Can I check one of these myself?

Yes. Open the vault, pick any verified finding, verify the signature, replay the reproducer against both builds. Thirty seconds. Please do.
